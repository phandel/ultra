05 Why these

The limiter is mechanical durability, not aerobic capacity.

Heart rate held flat at 122 bpm across 10.4 hours on the 40-miler with 6.3% decoupling — the engine is built. What breaks down is the chassis: cadence 156 spm (elite 170–180), ground contact 294 ms (elite <250), vertical ratio 10.3% (efficient <7).

Steep descents train exactly that, and this house is unusually well placed for it — you're at the top of the hill, so descent is free and climbing is unavoidable. Eccentric quad loading is what keeps the legs from failing at mile 35 on 9/5, and it's hard to find in Willow Glen.

Hiking costs about 5% of running-specific fitness over a week — mostly pace familiarity, not endurance — and buys time on feet with the loading pattern that matters. The one thing it doesn't train is cadence, so keep the short cadence-drill runs on the west side.
Caveats. Google's walking mode re-routes between my waypoints — exact on pavement, but its trail data at the reserve is thin and may draw odd connectors; trust the GPX files there. ASTER 30 m elevation smooths short features, so the driveway grade and the −23.7% pitch are both good-but-approximate. Reserve trail access and hours are worth a check before driving down.
