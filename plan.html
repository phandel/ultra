What explains the drift, then

Glycogen: ruled out (full power at the finish). Hypovolemia: ruled out (weight-neutral). Sodium: uncertain. Heat was the leading candidate — the break falls between 09:41 and 10:11 as the day warmed, and the curve plateaus at ~11% for the final 2.5 h rather than compounding, which is what thermal steady-state looks like. But 7/25 ran 8 h from 08:09 with zero drift, so heat alone doesn't explain it either. Most honest read: +7.4% over 5h34 is unremarkable for an ultra effort and may need no special explanation.

📋 The rules that matter

1. Cadence is the single biggest lever — and it's trainable in 5 weeks 157 spm with 274 ms ground contact and a 10.4% vertical ratio is one coherent problem: you're over-striding slightly, braking on each step, and bouncing. Quicker + shorter steps at the same pace fixes all three — and reduces the foot shear that blistered you at mile 40.

How to actually do it: set a metronome or 165-bpm playlist and match your footfalls. Don't try to run faster — keep the identical pace and just take smaller, quicker steps. It will feel unnatural for about 3 runs, then click. Aim for 162–166 by race day; chasing 170+ this late is not worth it.

Why it matters for the 50: your power dropped 11% over the 40-miler while your heart rate stayed flat. That's mechanical fatigue, and lower-impact mechanics is the direct fix. Every cadence drill in this plan is pointed at the last 15 miles of Sept 5.
2. Never 1 LMNT per 3 L That's ~220 mg sodium/hr — the hyponatremia setup, especially since you drink freely. Two packets per fill, three if it's hot.
3. Front-load less fluid than feels natural On the 40-miler you drank ~750 mL/hr in the cool pre-9 am hours, then needed ~1.45 L/hr in the heat and ran dry. Same total, better distribution: sip lightly early, drink hard from 10 am.
4. Eat more than you did on the 40 49 g/hr against 429 kcal/hr burned. Get to 65 g/hr — this is the most likely fix for the late-race power fade.
5. Nothing new after Aug 29 No new shoes, socks, foods, gels, or gear. Race day is execution, not experimentation.
6. After every long run: run-analyze.py Watch cadence trending toward 165, ground contact under 250 ms, and decoupling under 8%. The data tells you whether the plan is working before race day does.
